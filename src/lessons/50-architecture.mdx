<section class="title-center-body-left">
## <Icon name="i-tabler-stack-3" />Where does your app run?

- Your app process runs in a container, inside a Pod
- k3s runs that workload on a node
- Our node is your Ubuntu VM
- The VM has a guest kernel and virtual hardware
- A hypervisor runs the VM on host hardware

Point to those boundaries in your own setup.
</section>

<section class="title-center-body-left">
## <Icon name="i-tabler-box" />Containers and VMs

- Containers on our node share the Ubuntu guest kernel
- Containers isolate processes, filesystems, and networking
- A VM runs its own guest kernel
- The hypervisor provides virtual CPU, memory, and devices

Kubernetes manages workloads; the VM boundary remains.
</section>

<section class="title-center-body-left">
## <Icon name="i-tabler-shield" />Choose an isolation boundary

- Shared kernel: a kernel failure can affect many containers
- Separate VMs: separate guest kernels, with extra overhead
- Isolation also depends on configuration and privileges

Discuss: why might cloud tenants need separate guest kernels?
</section>

<section class="title-center-body-left">
## <Icon name="i-tabler-refresh" />Which failure did you test?

- Deleted app Pod: its ReplicaSet creates a replacement
- Bad readiness path: new Pod stays unready
- Old healthy Pods may still serve during that stalled rollout
- Lost VM: all three local app replicas are unavailable

Three replicas on one VM do not provide node resilience.
</section>

<section class="title-center-body-left">
## <Icon name="i-tabler-database" />The next practical milestone

Follow Lab 52’s guided setup:

- PostgreSQL, its Service, and persistent storage
- Secret references for the app’s database connection
- Insert and retrieve a distinct quote through the app
- Replace the database Pod, keeping its PVC
- Retrieve that same quote again

A successful `/health` request does not test these operations.
</section>

<section class="title-center-body-left">
## <Icon name="i-tabler-device-desktop" />What did persistence prove?

- The quote survives database Pod replacement
- The PVC uses storage on this VM
- Database replacement can interrupt service
- VM or disk loss needs a separate recovery design

Persistence across Pod replacement is a specific result.
It does not demonstrate backups or high availability.
</section>
